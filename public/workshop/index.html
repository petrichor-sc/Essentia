Smell the materials in front of you. Let a word, colour, texture or feeling lead you. A note has no single correct meaning.

01 · DISCOVER

Meet a note.

Choose one of the workshop materials. Smell gently from a blotter. Notice it before trying to name it.

02 · INTERPRET

Follow a feeling.

Does it feel crisp, soft, warm, distant—or something else? A memory is welcome; no memory is equally welcome.

Make it yours.

Use your impressions to guide your scent with the workshop facilitator. Give your creation a name, a colour, or a few words.

BEHIND THE ILLUSTRATIONS

Grounded in curiosity.
Guided by evidence.

The Himachal landscape is inspired by a personal memory, not a reconstruction of an identified location or jasmine species. Glowing dots in the air represent scent molecules; after the sensory neurons, amber pulses represent neural activity. The animations explain ideas. Particle sizes, colours, paths and speeds are illustrative. They do not measure an individual brain or predict a perfume’s performance.

  1. NIDCD · How your sense of smell works ↗Sensory neurons, receptor combinations and scent detection.
  2. Zhou et al., 2021 · Olfaction and hippocampal connectivity ↗Human brain imaging and electrophysiology; connectivity is not a guarantee of a particular emotional response.
  3. Willander & Larsson, 2006 · Autobiographical odour memory ↗A study in older adults, rather than a universal rule about childhood memories.
  4. Sobel et al., 2000 · Olfactory habituation ↗Neural responses can change during continuing exposure.
  5. Kasting & Saiyasombati, 2001 · Evaporation from skin ↗A model accounting for material properties, temperature, airflow and absorption.
  6. IFRA · Fragrance concentration terminology ↗Typical, overlapping industry ranges; labels are not internationally fixed concentration standards.
