Two ways in. One connected collection.

Explore places on the globe, or follow a body-and-mind topic. Open a study chapter to read cultural accounts, preparation details, evidence summaries, and their sources. The body figure is a navigation aid; a highlighted topic does not establish a treatment effect.

Different kinds of knowledge

Human research A study in people. Read the summary for the formulation, route, population, and limitations. A small study is not proof of clinical benefit.

Traditional use A documented cultural practice or a traditional-use medicinal assessment, identified in context. This is not equivalent to demonstrated efficacy.

Insufficient evidence This edition does not establish a particular health benefit. Missing evidence is stated rather than filled with a general wellness claim.

Where does a botanical come from?

A wild native range, an established cultivation region, a cultural tradition, and a supplier’s production location describe different things. The map retains these distinctions. Pins are representative regional reference points, not verified farm coordinates.

What is in this first edition?

The original website palette and supplier reference records are retained. Selected materials have expanded study chapters.

Catalogue production and extraction records are an editorial snapshot from 5 October 2026. Expanded chapter sources were checked on 18 October 2026. Supplier records do not establish the origin or specification of an individual bottle. Consult the linked source and the batch documentation.

Preparing and using aromatic materials

Plant preparations, distilled essential oils, absolutes, carrier oils, and formulated medicines are different materials. Follow product-specific instructions. Research on an oral medicine is not a recommendation to swallow an essential oil. Suitability, dilution, age restrictions, and sun exposure depend on the exact product and route.
